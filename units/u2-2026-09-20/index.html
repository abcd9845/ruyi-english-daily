Passage A · A Saturday Photography Walk

Last term, Lin Mei joined the photography club. She does not take photos only for fun—she also likes to have fun with friends after school. Every Saturday morning, she leaves home early with a small notebook. She writes down interesting ideas on each page. Then she walks near the river and looks for something different.

Lin Mei enjoys taking photos of nature. She likes high trees, quiet ground after rain, and birds that appear suddenly. One day, a weak little bird fell to the ground. Lin Mei held it carefully and asked a classmate for help. They took it to a pet shop. "Photography is an adventure," she says. "It teaches me to care." Her brother prefers to play volleyball and wants to join a club too, but Lin Mei would rather listen to soft music while she works.

At midnight she sometimes stays awake to check her photos again. Her mum says she should sleep, but Lin Mei shakes her head, then they shake hands and smile: "Almost finished. Just one more page in my notebook."

Passage B · More than a Hobby: Paper-cutting Club

Our school has a traditional Chinese paper-cutting club. Many students join it after school. Wang Hao is good at paper-cutting. He practises for thirty minutes every day. Next door, singers practice singing and learn to play an instrument before they stand on the stage. "I don't only want pretty pictures," he says. "I want to keep traditional art alive."

The club is different from the rock band next door. The band makes a loud sound; the paper-cutting room is quiet. Everybody taps the table softly and nods when a work looks magic. On festival days, they put red paper art on the stage wall. Visitors stop, smile, and feel joy.

Wang Hao's cousin plays volleyball and thinks paper-cutting is boring. Wang Hao does not get angry. He invites her to try once. She cuts a simple heart. Suddenly she understands: hobbies can sound different, but each can hit your heart.
